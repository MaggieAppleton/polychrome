Five layers, each a little higher off the table. Height is shown three ways at once: a deeper shadow, a slightly stronger hairline and a firmer spring. Nothing is flat and nothing floats without a reason. A thing is only as high as how temporary it is.

The stack

Ground is the table: warm grey, never white, holding no content of its own. Because it's the darkest surface, anything placed on it reads as paper.

Sheets are what you work on: a mission, a document, a thinking space. At most one sheet is ever in focus.

Cards rest on the ground or on a sheet: attention items, missions in a list.

Panels slide in from an edge: a conversation, a peek. They're always anchored to the edge they came from.

Popovers are menus and tooltips: the highest layer and the shortest-lived.

Attention and focus

The overview is the ground, with the attention log on its left and missions in motion beside it. Opening an item makes it a sheet in focus. The log and missions slide off, and the edges are left as empty table.

Controls in focus appear only while the pointer moves, and fade when it stops. The conversation slides in beside the sheet, pushing it aside rather than covering it, because talking is about the thing in front of you.

Attention that arrives during focus doesn't interrupt. It slides a slip of card up from under the bottom edge: a glyph and a count, no words. It moves once, never pulses, and is the only colour on screen. Bring the pointer near it and it rises far enough to read. It's calm because it doesn't move again, and unmistakable because it's physically there until you deal with it.
