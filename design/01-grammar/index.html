Polychrome is paper on a table. The table is the ground. Everything a person handles is a piece of paper on it, and its shape says what it is before any words do. There are two kinds of shape: discs are agents, the things that act, and rectangles are the things they act on.

Agent

A disc with a letter. The only round thing in Polychrome, so you can tell an actor from an object at a glance.

State is shown by motion, not by words. A working agent has an arc slowly circling it. An agent waiting on you has a still ring in the one accent colour. An idle agent fades.

In round 3, each agent may get its own hue. Here they're grey.

Mission

A card with a step track: one short bar per step. You read how far along it is, and whether it's stuck on you, before reading the title.

Done steps are grey, the current step breathes, and a step waiting on you takes the accent. The faces show who is working on it.

Attention item

Anything that needs a person. Five types, each with its own glyph, all the same size of card, so the log reads as one calm list.

Each item shows the thing itself: the options for a decision, a preview of what to review. You shouldn't have to open anything to know what's being asked.

An item you haven't seen yet tints its glyph. That's the only colour on it.

Subject

Items about the same thing rest on each other as a small pile. This is VISION.md's rule made physical: once you pick up interface design, the other interface design items come with it.

The pile's height is the count. A number appears only when there are more than three.

Attention log

One column. Open piles at the top, ordered by what Polychrome thinks should come first. Below a hairline sits what you've already handled, filed as decision records.

It's a log, not an inbox: nothing disappears. The past is how you trace why something was decided.

Decision record

What an item turns into once you decide. It shrinks, loses its shadow and gets a seal: it no longer asks for anything.

It keeps the choice and the reasons, and links to the mission it started.

Conversation

A tall panel, always beside something rather than instead of it. By default it's with the chief of staff; another agent's disc in the header means you're speaking to it directly.

No bubbles. Your words are plain text set in ink; an agent's carry its disc. Bookkeeping events are hidden.

Document

A portrait sheet of white paper with wide margins. Plans, research and specs. It has no toolbar; actions appear when you select text or open the sheet's menu.

Thinking space

Where one decision or problem gets its own interface. A patch of open ground where agents lay out what they made for it: comparisons, diagrams, design jigs, sketches. You can move the sheets around and draw between them.

A conversation docks at the edge so you can think aloud with an agent. Each space is made for one question and is archived when that question is decided.
